Build a camera gauge

Can a phone camera measure a washer well enough to accept or reject it? Here you turn a photo into millimetres, and find out where the errors come from. Everything runs in your browser: your photos never leave this device.

Before you start — kit and how to take the photo

Your group's kit

  • 10–15 washers from the batch, and one master washer marked with a pen
  • two phones (one is the light, one is the camera), a sheet of plain paper
  • the caliper (ISO 13385-1), at the instructor's desk — measure your master's outer and inner diameter there

Backlight: the best light for a silhouette

Open this page on the second phone or a tablet, press Lightbox, lay it flat, cover it with the paper and put the washers on the paper. The paper protects the screen and spreads the light evenly.

Taking the photo

  • Hold the camera parallel to the table. Rest it on two cups or a stack of books so it does not move between photos.
  • Use 2× zoom and step back: a longer focal length reduces perspective distortion.
  • Washers must not touch each other or the edge of the photo. The master can be anywhere.
  • Tap the screen to focus on the washers; avoid flash.

iPhone photo will not load on a laptop? Set Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible, or open this page on the phone and take the photo from here.

2Find the parts

Every pixel darker than the threshold counts as part, everything brighter as background. The suggested value sits in the valley between the two peaks (Otsu's method).

Threshold –
Why it matters. The edge of a washer in the photo is not a line: it is a ramp of grey pixels a few pixels wide. The threshold decides where in that ramp the edge is — and so the diameter you measure. Move it and watch the numbers in step 4.

3Calibrate: from pixels to millimetres

Measure your master's outer diameter with the caliper. Click the master in the photo, then type the caliper reading.

Put a ruler in the photo. Press the button, click two marks on it, and type the distance between them.

Why a master washer? Whatever the threshold does to the edge of the master, it does the same to every other washer, so calibrating on a part like the ones you measure cancels most of that error. A ruler cannot. But a threshold that makes the rim look bigger also makes the hole look smaller. Type the master's inner Ø too, and the page solves for the scale and for that edge shift together.

Under the hood — the same measurement in Python

For the curious: what this page does, in a few lines of Python. The notebook runs it step by step on the example photo or on yours, with plots — nothing to install, it runs on Google Colab (a free Google account is needed).

Open the notebook in Colab

import cv2, numpy as np
from scipy import ndimage

img = cv2.imread("washers.jpg", cv2.IMREAD_GRAYSCALE)        # grey levels, 0 = black, 255 = white
t, _ = cv2.threshold(img, 0, 255, cv2.THRESH_BINARY + cv2.THRESH_OTSU)   # the suggested threshold
metal  = img < t                                            # dark washers on a bright backlight
filled = ndimage.binary_fill_holes(metal)                   # each washer with its hole filled in
lab, n = ndimage.label(filled)                              # give every washer a number

idx   = np.arange(1, n + 1)
a_out = ndimage.sum(filled, lab, idx)                       # area inside the outer edge
a_in  = a_out - ndimage.sum(metal, lab, idx)                # area of the hole
edge  = np.unique(np.r_[lab[0], lab[-1], lab[:, 0], lab[:, -1]])
keep  = (a_out > 2000) & ~np.isin(idx, edge)                # no specks, nothing cut by the edge
od_px = 2 * np.sqrt(a_out[keep] / np.pi)                    # diameter of a circle of the same area
id_px = 2 * np.sqrt(a_in[keep] / np.pi)

master = 0                                  # index of your master washer in the list
mm_per_px = 15.94 / od_px[master]           # its caliper reading, in mm
for k in range(len(od_px)):
    print(f"part {k}: outer {od_px[k] * mm_per_px:.3f} mm, inner {id_px[k] * mm_per_px:.3f} mm")

Two ideas carry the whole method. A diameter from an area (Ø = 2√(A/π)) averages over hundreds of edge pixels, so it is far steadier than measuring from one edge to the other. And the hole is the background enclosed by the part, found by filling the part in and subtracting.

Standards behind this exercise. ISO 7089 — the washers and their tolerances · ISO 13385-1 — callipers, the reference instrument · ISO 5725-2 — repeatability: same operator, instrument and conditions, short time · JCGM 100 (GUM) — expressing measurement uncertainty.
