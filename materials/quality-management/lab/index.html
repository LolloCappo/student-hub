Can a phone camera inspect a batch of washers?

On production lines, cameras check parts that no person could check one by one. In this session you build such a check yourself: you turn your phone into a measuring instrument, find out how good it really is, and use it to decide whether a real batch of washers is acceptable. No installation and no programming — everything runs in your browser, and your photos never leave your device.

One story in three steps — what the camera records, then millimetres, then a decision. Do them in order: each exercise uses what the previous one showed.

The session

  • 0:00The question, the batch and the groups. Each group measures its master washer with the caliper.
  • 0:15Exercise 1 — inside your camera
  • 0:40Exercise 2 — build a camera gauge; at least three photos of the same washers
  • 1:25Break. Every group sends its CSV file; the class data are merged.
  • 1:40Exercise 3 — accept or reject the batch, on the whole class's data
  • 2:25Each group gives its verdict and defends it. Why do groups disagree?

Each exercise ends with a few questions. Answer them as a group: they are what we discuss at the end.

What to bring

  • Your phone, charged. Two per group: one is the camera, the other the light.
  • A laptop per group if you can. A phone alone works too, just on a small screen.
  • Before the session, open this page on both and press Try the example in exercise 2.

On the desk for each group

  • about 15 numbered washers from the batch, and one marked master washer
  • white paper, a ruler, and the caliper at the instructor's desk

iPhone photos will not open on a laptop? Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible, or open the exercise on the phone and take the photo from there.

The standards you will meet

  • ISO 7089 — plain washers, normal series, product grade A: the parts and their tolerances
  • ISO 13385-1 — callipers: the reference instrument
  • ISO 5725-2 — repeatability and reproducibility of a measurement method
  • JCGM 100 (GUM) — how to express measurement uncertainty
  • ISO 14253-1 — decision rules for proving conformity or nonconformity
  • ISO 22514-2 — process capability and performance
  • ISO 3951-1 — lot acceptance by sampling, by variables
  • ISO 12233, ISO 15739 — resolution and noise of digital cameras
  • EMVA 1288 — characterisation of machine-vision cameras (industry standard)
  • ISO/IEC 10918-1 — JPEG compression
